# This Too Shall Pass

There's an old story, told in enough different places that nobody really owns it anymore, about a king who summoned his wisest advisor and asked for something strange: a ring, engraved with words that would be true no matter what happened to him. Words that would humble him in his greatest triumph, and comfort him in his darkest hour — the same words, doing both jobs.

The advisor thought for a long time. Then he had the jeweler engrave four words inside the band: *this too shall pass.*

The story shows up almost word for word in Persian poetry, in Jewish folklore, in Sufi teaching, in a hundred grandmothers' kitchens who never read any of those sources and didn't need to. A saying that keeps getting rediscovered independently, in cultures that never spoke to each other, usually survives because it's just true.

Here's the part worth sitting with: whatever you're feeling right now — however solid it seems, however much it feels like the new permanent weather of your life — is on the exact same clock as every feeling you've ever had before it. The heartbreak that once felt like it would never lift, lifted. The dread before something you were sure would break you, faded once the thing actually happened and turned out to be survivable, or didn't happen at all. The rage that felt righteous and endless cooled, the way rage always does, whether or not you wanted it to.

None of this means the feeling isn't real right now. It is. The ring doesn't say the feeling is fake — it says the feeling has a shelf life, the same as the one before it and the one before that.

Try this, if you want: reach back exactly one year and see if you can find a feeling that gripped you that hard. Something that felt unbearable, or unshakeable, or certain to define the rest of your life. Most people have to search for it. Some can't find it at all — it's just gone, worn smooth by time, the way every feeling eventually is.

That's not a trick of memory. That's the ring, working exactly the way it was engraved to.
