# Dodge the Punch

Thoughts can be like weather.

Some are sunny. Some are cloudy. Some drift through like a little rain and disappear before you pay much attention to them. And then there are the thoughts that don't feel like weather at all.
They hit like a punch.

*I'm going to lose everything.*
*Nobody really likes me.*
*I've wasted too much time.*
*It's too late.*

These thoughts don't politely ask whether you'd like to think about them. They arrive with force. Sometimes they come with enough momentum that, before you even notice what happened, you're already following them down the road.

MentalFu isn't asking you to solve the thought right now.

Sometimes you can't. Sometimes the thought is too strong. Sometimes the problem behind it is real. Sometimes it's two in the morning and there is absolutely nothing useful you can do about it until tomorrow.

That's okay. This training is simpler than that.

Learn to recognize the punch. Then practice the dodge.

A dodge might sound like:

*I can worry about this later.*
*I wonder if that's actually true.*
*This too shall pass.*
*I don't need to solve this right now.*

You're not proving the thought wrong. You're not pretending everything is fine. You're not trying to make the thought disappear. You're practicing the small space between **a thought arriving** and **automatically following it**.

## That is the MOVE.

Sometimes the best thing to do with a punch is not block it, fight it, or stand there trying to prove that it shouldn't hurt. Sometimes you just move.

## If the punch is happening right now

Good. You already have a training partner.

Notice one thought that keeps trying to pull you in. Don't wrestle with it. Don't solve it. Practice one dodge.

If the thought comes back thirty seconds later, that's another rep. If you dodge once and then get pulled back into it for twenty minutes, the rep still counts. You noticed the punch. You moved.

Next time, maybe you'll notice it a little sooner.

That's training.

## If the sky is blue today

You don't need to be in trouble to practice. Martial artists don't wait until someone is throwing a real punch before they learn how to dodge one. Use a small thought that appears during your day.

*I shouldn't have said that.*
*What if tomorrow goes badly?*
*They're probably annoyed with me.*

Nothing enormous. Nothing you need to dig up from your past.
Just notice the thought and practice moving around it.
*Maybe. I don't need to follow that right now.*

That's a practice rep.

The goal of today's Kata is not to become someone who never has intrusive thoughts.

The goal is to become someone who starts recognizing when one is coming.

See the punch.

Practice the dodge.

One rep is enough.
